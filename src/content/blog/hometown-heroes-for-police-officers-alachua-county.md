## What is the Florida Hometown Heroes Housing Program?

The Florida Hometown Heroes Housing Program is a state-administered initiative designed to help frontline and community workers purchase homes in Florida. It provides a zero-percent, deferred second mortgage — a subordinate loan with no monthly payment that is repaid when you sell, refinance, or move out of the home — to help cover down payment and closing costs when paired with an eligible first mortgage. Stairway Mortgage is not the official program administrator; this site helps frontline workers understand and access the program alongside their first mortgage.

The program is open to workers in a wide range of occupations, and [checking whether your specific role qualifies](/eligible-occupations) is a useful first step before going further in the process.

## What are the eligibility requirements for law enforcement?

To potentially qualify as a law enforcement officer, you would generally need to meet all of the following:

- Be employed full-time in an eligible law enforcement role in Florida
- Meet the program's household income limits for your county
- Be purchasing a primary residence — not a second home or investment property
- Meet first-time homebuyer requirements (with some exceptions)
- Work with a participating lender to secure an approved first mortgage product
- Complete a homebuyer education course if required

Because income limits and purchase price caps vary by county, what applies in Alachua County may differ from other parts of the state. [Reviewing income limit guidance](/income-limits) specific to your situation is worth doing early.

## How does using Hometown Heroes work in Alachua County?

In Alachua County, the process follows the same general steps as the rest of Florida, but local market conditions do shape how the program fits your situation. Gainesville and surrounding communities in the county span a range of home price points, and the assistance amount you may receive through the program is tied to your first mortgage amount.

Here is a general overview of how the process tends to work:

1. **Confirm your occupation qualifies.** Law enforcement is listed, but your specific role and employer may need to be verified.
2. **Check income eligibility.** Program income limits are based on area median income and vary by county.
3. **Get pre-qualified for a first mortgage.** The second mortgage must be paired with an approved first mortgage product.
4. **Find a home and go under contract.** The program applies to the purchase of a primary residence.
5. **Reserve your program funds.** Funds are allocated on a first-come, first-served basis once your file is in order.
6. **Close on your home.** The deferred second mortgage is recorded alongside your first mortgage at closing.

## What should police officers watch out for during the process?

A few common points of confusion are worth addressing before you get started.

First, the assistance is not free money. The deferred second mortgage will need to be repaid when you sell the home, refinance, or no longer occupy it as your primary residence. Planning around that future obligation matters.

Second, program funds can run out. Allocations are made available on a first-come, first-served basis during each funding cycle, so acting when you are ready — rather than waiting — may work in your favor.

Third, not every lender participates in this program. You need to work with an approved participating lender who can originate both the first mortgage and coordinate the second mortgage. This is not something every mortgage company handles.
