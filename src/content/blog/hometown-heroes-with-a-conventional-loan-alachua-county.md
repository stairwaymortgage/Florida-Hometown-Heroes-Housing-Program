Yes, you can use the Florida Hometown Heroes Housing Program alongside a conventional loan. The state program is designed to work with multiple first mortgage types, and a conventional loan is one of them. For eligible frontline workers in Alachua County, pairing these two options could make homeownership more reachable by reducing the upfront cash needed to close.

## What is the Florida Hometown Heroes Housing Program?

The Florida Hometown Heroes Housing Program is a state-run initiative administered through the Florida Housing Finance Corporation. It is designed to help eligible frontline and community workers purchase a home by offering a second mortgage that covers a portion of the down payment and closing costs. This second mortgage carries no interest and requires no monthly payments, but it is a repayable deferred loan, not a grant or free money. Repayment is due when you sell, refinance, or otherwise transfer the property.

## Can you use Hometown Heroes with a conventional loan?

Yes, a conventional loan is one of the eligible first mortgage types under the Hometown Heroes program. A conventional loan is a mortgage that is not backed by a federal agency such as the FHA, VA, or USDA. Instead, it follows guidelines set by Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac and is funded by private lenders.

When paired with the Hometown Heroes second mortgage, a conventional first mortgage may allow eligible buyers to enter the market with less upfront cash while still using a widely available loan structure. The second mortgage from the program layers on top of your conventional loan and is held separately.

Not every lender participates in the Hometown Heroes program, so it matters to work with an originator who is already approved to originate these loans. Stairway Mortgage works with buyers specifically seeking this combination.

## What are the general eligibility requirements?

To use the Hometown Heroes program with a conventional loan, you typically need to meet requirements in several areas. Here is a general checklist of what the program commonly looks for:

- You must work full-time in a qualifying eligible occupation
- You must be a first-time homebuyer or meet any alternative buyer criteria the program allows
- Your household income must fall at or under the county-specific limit
- The home must be your primary residence
- Your conventional loan must meet the program's guidelines for loan type and loan amount
- You must meet the credit and debt-to-income standards required by both the first mortgage and the second mortgage program
- You must complete any required homebuyer education

## How does the second mortgage affect my loan structure?

The Hometown Heroes second mortgage is a deferred, zero-interest loan. That means it does not accrue interest over time and does not require a monthly payment during the life of your first mortgage. However, it is not forgiven. When you sell the home, refinance the first mortgage, or transfer the title, the second mortgage balance becomes due.

For buyers focused on the long-term, this structure means the assistance functions more like a deferred obligation than a windfall. It reduces what you need at closing, which could allow you to preserve savings or qualify for a home sooner. But it is important to factor the second mortgage balance into your long-term planning, especially if you anticipate selling or refinancing within a few years.
