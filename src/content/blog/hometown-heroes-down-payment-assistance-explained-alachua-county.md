The Florida Hometown Heroes Housing Program works by pairing a qualifying first mortgage with a deferred second mortgage — structured at zero interest — that provides funds toward your down payment and closing costs. Eligible frontline workers in Alachua County may access this state program through a participating lender. The second mortgage is not forgiven; it is repaid when you sell, refinance, or pay off the home.

If you are a teacher in Gainesville, a firefighter in Alachua County, or a healthcare worker anywhere in the county, coming up with a down payment on top of everyday expenses can feel like an impossible hurdle. The Florida Hometown Heroes Housing Program exists specifically to help community workers like you bridge that gap — and understanding how it actually works is the first step.

## How does the Florida Hometown Heroes down payment assistance work?

The program provides a deferred second mortgage — meaning payments on this portion are postponed — that you receive at closing alongside a qualifying first mortgage. A deferred second mortgage is a loan secured by your home on which no monthly payments are due while you live there, but the full principal balance becomes due upon a triggering event such as a sale or refinance. The funds may be used toward your down payment, closing costs, or both, depending on your first mortgage type.

Because the assistance is a loan and not a grant, it does not disappear. When you eventually sell or refinance, the amount is repaid to the program. This structure allows the state to recycle funds and help future Florida workers.

## Who qualifies for the program?

Eligibility is generally limited to Florida frontline and community workers who meet the program's occupational, income, purchase price, and homebuyer requirements.

Eligible occupations often include:

- Licensed educators and school support staff
- Registered nurses, paramedics, and other healthcare professionals
- Law enforcement officers and corrections officers
- Firefighters and EMTs
- Active military members and veterans
- Child care workers and social workers
- Other qualifying community roles listed on the [eligible occupations page](/eligible-occupations)

Beyond occupation, you generally need to:

1. Be purchasing a primary residence in Florida — in this case, in Alachua County
2. Meet the program's income requirements (visit the [income limits page](/income-limits) for general guidance)
3. Be a first-time homebuyer, defined as not having owned a primary residence in the past three years (some military exceptions may apply)
4. Obtain a qualifying first mortgage through a participating lender
5. Complete a homebuyer education course if required

## What kind of first mortgage does the program require?

The Hometown Heroes second mortgage must be paired with a specific type of qualifying first mortgage — it cannot be added to just any loan. Acceptable first mortgage types often include government-backed options such as FHA, VA, USDA, and conventional financing that meets program guidelines.

Each first mortgage type has its own requirements around credit, property condition, and loan limits, so your overall picture — including your credit profile and the home you are purchasing — will shape which first mortgage fits. ## What should I do before applying?

Preparing ahead of time can make the process smoother and may help you move quickly when funds are available.

- **Check your occupation:** Confirm your role appears on the [eligible occupations list](/eligible-occupations) before investing time in the application process.
- **Review your credit:** Lenders will pull a credit report. Knowing where you stand lets you address any issues early.
- **Gather documentation:** Pay stubs, tax returns, employment verification, and bank statements are typically needed.
- **Complete homebuyer education:** Some configurations of the program require a HUD-approved homebuyer education course.
- **Connect with a participating lender:** The second mortgage is issued through the lender alongside your first mortgage, so choosing the right originator matters.

## Is this the right program for frontline workers buying in Alachua County?

For eligible workers, the Hometown Heroes program could meaningfully reduce the upfront cash needed to purchase a home. Because the assistance is a deferred second mortgage rather than out-of-pocket savings, it may allow you to buy sooner than you would by saving independently.

That said, it is not the right fit for every situation. If you plan to sell or refinance within a few years, you will need to account for repaying the second mortgage at that time. A conversation with a knowledgeable loan originator can help you weigh whether this structure aligns with your plans.
