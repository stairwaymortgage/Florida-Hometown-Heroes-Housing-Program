Eligible participants in Florida's Hometown Heroes Housing Program are full-time employed frontline and community workers — including teachers, nurses, firefighters, law enforcement officers, EMTs, corrections officers, and active-duty military or veterans — who are purchasing a primary residence in Florida for the first time and who meet the program's income and credit requirements. The program is administered through Florida Housing and is available in every county, including Alachua County.

## Who qualifies for Florida's Hometown Heroes program?

To qualify, you generally need to work full-time in an eligible public-serving occupation, meet household income guidelines, and be purchasing a home as your primary residence. Most applicants must also be first-time homebuyers — meaning they have not owned a primary home in the past three years — though certain military veterans may be exempt from that requirement.

Beyond occupation and income, lenders will look at your credit profile, debt levels, and the property itself. The program is not a standalone product; it works alongside a qualifying first mortgage, which means you need to meet the requirements of that loan as well.

If you are a frontline worker in Alachua County wondering whether you might fit the criteria, reviewing the [list of eligible occupations](/eligible-occupations) is a natural first step.

## What occupations are covered?

The program covers a wide range of community-facing careers. If you work in one of the fields below on a full-time basis, you may be eligible:

- **Education:** K-12 teachers, instructional staff, school counselors, and administrators
- **Healthcare:** Nurses, physicians, nursing assistants, therapists, pharmacists, and other licensed healthcare providers
- **Law enforcement:** Police officers, sheriff's deputies, and other sworn officers
- **Fire services:** Firefighters and fire inspectors
- **Emergency medical services:** EMTs and paramedics
- **Corrections:** Corrections officers and juvenile justice staff
- **Military and veterans:** Active-duty service members and honorably discharged veterans purchasing a primary residence
- **Child care and social services:** Licensed child care workers and certain social service professionals

This is not an exhaustive list. If your career is in public service or community support and you are not sure whether it qualifies, the [eligible occupations page](/eligible-occupations) has a fuller breakdown, and Jim Blackburn, NMLS #1072866, can help you work through the specifics.

## How does this program work in Alachua County?

In Alachua County, the program operates the same way it does across Florida — eligible workers pair the Hometown Heroes second mortgage with a qualifying first mortgage to help cover down payment and certain closing costs. Gainesville, the county seat, is among the local communities where eligible buyers have used this program to purchase a home.

The assistance comes in the form of a deferred second mortgage (a loan secured by your home that does not require monthly payments) at zero percent interest. It is not a grant or free money — it is a loan that must be repaid when you sell the home, refinance your first mortgage, or pay it off entirely.

Alachua County has a diverse housing market, and the program is designed to give community workers a realistic path to homeownership without requiring them to save an entire down payment out-of-pocket. You can explore more county-specific context through the [Florida county housing programs](/florida-county-housing-programs) section.

## What financial requirements do I need to meet?

Meeting the occupational requirement is only part of the picture. The program also asks that you meet household income limits, which vary based on the county where you are buying and the size of your household. Because these figures are set by the state and updated periodically, the most current numbers should be confirmed directly with your lender. You can also get a general sense of where you might fall by visiting the [income limits page](/income-limits).

Your credit profile matters, too. The first mortgage you pair with the program will have its own minimum credit score requirements, and your debt-to-income ratio will be reviewed. A lender who works with this program regularly can walk you through how those pieces interact.

## Can I estimate how much assistance I might receive?

The program ties its assistance amount to the first mortgage, so the exact figure depends on the loan product you use and the purchase price of the home. Because the program does not publish a flat dollar amount, the most practical approach is to model your specific scenario with a loan originator.

Using the [loan calculator](/loan-calculator) on this site could give you a starting point for understanding your overall mortgage picture before you sit down with a lender.

## What documents will I likely need to gather?

Being organized before you apply may help move the process along. Here is a checklist of items most applicants will need:

1. **Proof of employment:** A recent pay stub and, in some cases, a letter from your employer confirming your full-time status and eligible occupation
2. **Income documentation:** W-2s from the past two years and recent federal tax returns
3. **Credit authorization:** You will need to consent to a credit pull as part of the mortgage process
4. **Government-issued ID:** A valid driver's license or passport
5. **Bank statements:** Typically two to three months of recent statements for all accounts
6. **Purchase contract:** Once you have a property under contract, that document will be required
7. **Military documentation (if applicable):** A DD-214 or current orders if you are using a veteran or active-duty exemption
